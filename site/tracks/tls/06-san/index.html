the names a certificate is valid for: SAN

tls · level 06 of 23 · san

the ticket

Lesson: the CN in the subject is only a label. What browsers and curl actually compare against the address you typed is a list inside the certificate called the SAN (subject alternative name): DNS:shop.local, possibly several. No SAN, or a different name in it → "hostname mismatch", even if the CN is right. The signing step decides what goes in. Extras like the SAN come from a small text file passed with -extfile: subjectAltName=DNS:shop.local Try it (a request shop.csr for my.key, and ca.crt/ca.key, are in the lab): cd ~/.k8s-wargame/tls/lab openssl x509 -req -in shop.csr -CA ca.crt -CAkey ca.key -CAcreateserial -days 30 -out nosan.crt openssl x509 -noout -ext subjectAltName -in nosan.crt "No extensions" — no names at all echo subjectAltName=DNS:demo.local > demo.ext openssl x509 -req -in shop.csr -CA ca.crt -CAkey ca.key -CAcreateserial -days 30 -extfile demo.ext -out demo.crt openssl x509 -noout -ext subjectAltName -in demo.crt DNS:demo.local Your task: sign shop.csr with the CA into ~/.k8s-wargame/tls/lab/shop.crt so that its SAN contains DNS:shop.local (write the ext file ~/.k8s-wargame/tls/lab/san.ext first). Work in ~/.k8s-wargame/tls/lab. Check yourself: wg check stuck? wg hint
